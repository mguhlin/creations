Conceptual Framework

RAT & PICRAT

Replacement, Amplification, Transformation, and the student-centered extension.

The RAT model, developed by Dr. Joan Hughes in 1998, is a self-assessment framework for understanding technology's role in teaching, learning, and curriculum. It asks teachers to judge an instance of technology use against three categories.

▸

The Three Categories

Replacement

Technology serves the same purpose as prior practice, with no real change to instruction, learning, or curriculum.

Amplification

Technology increases the efficiency and productivity of the same task, without changing its fundamental nature.

Transformation

Technology invents new instruction, new learning processes, or new curriculum that would not otherwise exist.

To assess a technology's contribution, you consider an instance of use across three themes: instructional methods, student learning processes, and curriculum goals.

▸

PICRAT: Adding the Student

PICRAT extends RAT by adding the missing student component. It combines two models: PIC describes the student's relationship to the technology (Passive, Interactive, Creative), and RAT describes the technology's impact on the teacher's prior practice (Replacement, Amplification, Transformation).

The goal is the bottom-right of the grid, where students are creative and technology transforms practice. Like SAMR, PICRAT is a conceptual model useful for discussion, but it may not ensure measurable gains in student outcomes, so use it as a guide rather than a scorecard.
