Part 5

Tips: Practical Technology Planning

"We're not going to have deeper learning for all students if we don't have deeper learning leadership for all teachers." — Barnett Berry
▸

Five Pillars of a Technology Plan

Sound technology planning rests on a handful of recurring decisions. Keep these five in view as you build or revise a plan.

1 · Long-Term Planning

Look past the current budget cycle. Tie technology decisions to a multi-year instructional vision.

2 · Identifying Needs

Name the instructional and infrastructure needs before naming the tools that might meet them.

3 · Obsolescence & Replacement

Plan for the end of life of every device and system, with a funded replacement cycle.

4 · Budget & Sources

Know your funding streams, including bonds, the federal E-Rate program, and the state Technology and Instructional Materials Allotment.

5 · Critical Components

Decide which elements are non-negotiable for the plan to succeed, and protect them.

▸

Seven Practical Tips

Tip 1 Create a knowledge management site

Give your work a home. A single, well-organized site to house plans, data, and the content you create makes everything else easier to find, share, and sustain.

Tip 2 Conduct an assessment

Try "two stars and a wish." Ask each campus and department for two things technology is doing right and one thing they wish could improve. Then prioritize the wishes and celebrate the stars. Aim to finish this within your first three months on the job.

Learn more about stars and wishes.

Tip 3 Ask "what do you really do?"

Ask each member of the technology team to write down what they actually do. The result shows who does what, surfaces gaps, and helps you plan ahead for growth in staffing and resources.

Tip 4 Establish benchmarks

Run a needs assessment, instructional, infrastructure, or both. It is hard to show progress with no baseline. Use your benchmarks to set Key Performance Indicators for the future.

Learn more about KPIs.

Tip 5 Make a Top Ten list of needs

It is easy to get lost in the weeds. Turn your assessment data into a Top Ten list of priorities, and use it at every opportunity to help stakeholders understand what the district actually needs.

Tip 6 Use project management for everything

Project management belongs in every district undertaking, technology projects included. Put your tools to work tracking scope, owners, and deadlines so initiatives finish rather than stall.

Learn more about project management.

Tip 7 Shape a shared vision

A technology vision that staff helped write is a vision they will defend. Facilitated visioning, where stakeholders name what they want technology to make possible, produces a plan with broad ownership.

▸

Bonus Tip

Track state and national legislative priorities. What lawmakers fund and require shapes what is possible. Following these priorities helps you focus your planning and build a sense of urgency when you need it. For Texas, the TCEA advocacy page is a good place to start.
