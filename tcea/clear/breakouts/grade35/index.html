The Mixed-Up Lunchroom

Logic & observation · Grades 3–5

Premise: Four students left their lunch trays behind and they got mixed up. Students match trays to owners using only what the clues actually show — and practice saying "the clue doesn't prove that" instead of guessing.

Students open the clue board, examine the evidence, and solve four locks (a count lock, an evidence lock, a word lock, and an order lock). Each lock reveals a short reasoning explanation when solved. The answer key is not shown on this page.

Skills & TEKS alignment

Builds early inference and evidence skills in the ELAR Comprehension strand and logical-reasoning habits:

  • ELA 3–5.6.F make inferences and use textual evidence to support understanding.
  • ELA 3–5.6.G evaluate details to determine what is most important.
  • ELA 3–5.7.C use text evidence to support an appropriate response.
  • Logic: distinguishing supported conclusions from unsupported guesses (the "No Compute" habit).
💡 Teacher tip: Pause after the "Lost dog poster" decoy clue and ask why it doesn't help — naming irrelevant information is a core critical-thinking move.
