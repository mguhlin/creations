Course Builder Instructions

Purpose and Goals:

  • Assume the role of an expert online course builder.
  • Develop a comprehensive course curriculum based on provided files (megaprompt.txt, example0.txt (the best version), example1.txt, example2.txt, example3.txt).
  • Generate course content that aligns with the format and organization of the provided materials.
  • Elaborate on the provided content as needed to create a complete and engaging course.

Behaviors and Rules:

  1. File Review:

a) Begin by thoroughly reviewing the provided files (megaprompt.txt, example1.txt, example2.txt, example3.txt) to understand the course topic, structure, and content.

  1. Content Generation:

a) Generate course content that incorporates the format and organization of the provided materials.

b) Elaborate on the provided content as needed to ensure clarity, completeness, and engagement.

c) Use clear and concise language that is appropriate for the target audience.

d) Employ a variety of teaching methods, such as examples, analogies, and real-world applications.

e) Incorporate visuals, such as images, diagrams, and videos, to enhance learning.

f) Include interactive elements, such as quizzes, exercises, and discussion prompts, to encourage active participation.

  1. Copyright Check:

a) Conduct online searches for the course name and instructions to identify any potential copyright issues.

b) If copyright concerns are identified, modify the course content to ensure originality and avoid infringement.

Overall Tone:

  • Maintain a professional and knowledgeable tone throughout the course.
  • Exhibit enthusiasm and passion for the subject matter.
  • Foster a supportive and encouraging learning environment.

Adhere to the TCEA Style Guide below

AVOID the following:

Ampersand (&) use; instead use “and”

Placement of two spaces after a period at the end of a sentence

Overuse of exclamation marks (!)

Typing the full hyperlink and then linking it (https://tcea.org )

Putting periods at the ends of bulleted or numbered lists unless they are complete sentences

DO the following

Use the Oxford comma

Capitalize if the product being taught is normally capitalized

Hyperlink text of the link rather than adding the link itself (TCEA Courses)

Bold links

Explain common abbreviations like LMS, IEP, etc.

Write out numbers up to ten (“Take three of these….” instead of “Take 3 of these…”)

Capitalize the first letter of each bullet or numbered list item

If a punctuation mark is needed to separate items surrounded by quotation marks, put it inside the quotation marks. (Save the file and click "Open." instead of Save the File and click "Open".)

Rely on second person (e.g. “you” perspective and “you, your, yours, yourself, yourselves”)

1.1.1 - Adding Closed Captioning to Google Slides

“Closed captioning in Slides can help audience members … who are deaf or hard of hearing, but it can also be useful for audience members without hearing loss who are listening in noisy auditoriums or rooms with poor sound settings,” G Suite Accessibility Software Engineers Laura D’Aquila and Abigail Klein wrote in a blog post recently. “Closed captioning can also be a benefit when the presenter is speaking a non-native language or is not projecting their voice.” Learn more.

[mod111.mp4]

Script: Welcome to this tutorial on adding closed captioning to Google Slides. Today, we'll explore how this feature can enhance accessibility and improve the overall presentation experience. To begin, open your Google Slides presentation. Navigate to the "Tools" menu and select "Closed captions." A panel will appear on the right side of your screen. Here, you can choose your microphone and language settings. When you're ready to present, click the "Start" button in the captions panel. As you speak, your words will be transcribed in real-time at the bottom of your slide. This feature is particularly beneficial for audience members who are deaf or hard of hearing, as well as those in noisy environments or non-native speakers. Remember, you can adjust the caption settings, such as font size and position, to best suit your presentation needs. By incorporating closed captions, you're making your content more accessible and inclusive for all audience members.

1.1.2 - Adding Closed Captioning to Microsoft PowerPoint

In PowerPoint for Windows, you can add closed captions or subtitles to videos and audio files in your presentations. Adding closed captions makes your presentation accessible to a larger audience, including people with hearing disabilities and those who speak languages other than the one in your video. Learn more.

[mod112.mp4]

Script: In this video, we'll walk through the process of adding closed captions to videos and audio files in Microsoft PowerPoint for Windows. First, insert your video or audio file into your PowerPoint slide. Select the media file, then go to the "Playback" tab on the ribbon. Look for the "Captions" button and click on it. You'll see options to add captions from a file or to create new ones. If you have a pre-existing caption file, you can easily import it. Otherwise, choose to create new captions. For new captions, PowerPoint will open a window where you can type or paste your caption text. You can set the timing for each caption to sync with your media. Adding closed captions not only makes your presentation more accessible to those with hearing disabilities but also helps non-native speakers better understand your content.

1.1.3 - Use Flipgrid to Generate Audio Transcriptions from Audio and Video Clips That are 10 Minutes or Less

Flipgrid offers storage of up to 10-minute videos. What many may not realize is that it will also convert audio/video files you upload and, in time, offer you closed captioning (a.k.a. audio transcription) for those files.

[mod113.mp4]

Script: _Today, we're exploring how to use Flipgrid to generate audio transcriptions for short audio and video clips. Start by logging into your Flipgrid account and creating a new topic. When uploading your audio or video file, ensure it's no longer than 10 minutes, as this is Flipgrid's maximum storage limit. After uploading, Flipgrid will automatically begin processing your file. This includes generating an audio transcription, also known as closed captioning. The processing time may vary depending on the length of your file. Once complete, you'll be able to view and edit the transcription. This feature is incredibly useful for creating accessible content, especially for students who may prefer or require written text alongside audio or video materials. _

Problem

Your school district has adopted a new digital textbook which is not available in the native language of a student. You have found that the digital textbook is fully compatible with the Chromebooks issued to students. How would you go about obtaining read-aloud support in a foreign language for a student?

Solution

[mod121.mp4]

This video describes how students can use the unofficial Chrome browser add-on for Immersive Reader to translate content on a web page.

Script: Welcome to this tutorial on using the unofficial Chrome browser add-on for Immersive Reader to translate content on a web page. This tool can be incredibly helpful for students who need read-aloud support in a foreign language. To begin, we'll need to install the Immersive Reader extension for Chrome. Open the Chrome Web Store and search for "Use Immersive Reader on Websites." Click "Add to Chrome" to install the extension. Once installed, navigate to the digital textbook's web page. Select the text you want to translate by clicking and dragging your cursor over it. Then, right-click on the selected text and choose "Help me read this" from the context menu. The selected text will now appear in the Immersive Reader interface. At the bottom of the screen, you'll see a "Translate" option. Click on it and choose the desired language from the dropdown menu. Not only will the text be translated, but you can also have it read aloud in the chosen language. To do this, simply click the play button at the bottom of the screen. Remember, you can adjust various settings to enhance the reading experience. These include text size, font, background color, and line focus. You can also use the picture dictionary feature for visual learners. By using this tool, students can access the content of their digital textbooks in their native language, complete with read-aloud support. This can significantly improve comprehension and engagement with the material. Keep in mind that while this extension is unofficial, it provides a valuable solution for multilingual classrooms and students learning in a non-native language

Glossary

This glossary provides you with a comprehensive understanding of key terms and tools used in the course, facilitating effective integration of AI into their teaching practices.

Glossary of Terms

Adaptive Learning: AI-driven technology that adjusts educational content based on individual student performance to create personalized learning experiences.

Algorithm: A set of rules or instructions given to an AI system to help it learn and make decisions.

Artificial Intelligence (AI): A branch of computer science focused on creating systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence.

Blog.Pincel: An AI app that can detect text from images, analyze or summarize it, and provide insights or descriptions, enhancing learning experiences by transforming how we interact with visuals.

Brisk: An AI tool used for generating questions and facilitating discussions in educational settings.

Chalkie.ai: An AI tool used for creating engaging learning materials and interactive content.

Deep Learning: A type of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks to enable digital systems to learn and make decisions based on unstructured, unlabeled data.

DeepL: An AI-powered translation tool that supports multilingual students by providing accurate translations.

Generative AI: Artificial intelligence that can create original content—such as text, images, video, audio, or software code—in response to a user’s prompt or request.

Gamma.app: A tool for creating compelling visual presentations and data visualizations.

Khanmigo: An AI tool designed to support students with special needs through personalized learning experiences and accessibility features.

Leo (withleo.ai): An AI platform that provides insights for designing assessments and analyzing student performance data.

Machine Learning (ML): A subset of AI that uses algorithms to learn from data and make predictions without being explicitly programmed.

MagicSchool: An educational platform that uses AI to create personalized learning experiences and helps educators understand the basics of machine learning.

Markerpen: An AI tool for grading handwritten tests efficiently.

Natural Language Processing (NLP): A field of AI that focuses on the interaction between computers and humans through natural language.

Napkin: A tool for creating flowcharts and mind maps using AI for enhanced visualization.

SchoolAI: A platform that integrates AI tools across different curriculum areas to enhance teaching and learning experiences.

SubEasy: A text-to-speech application designed to support accessibility in educational settings.

TalkPal: An AI tool for language acquisition that provides speaking practice through simulated conversations.

TeacherServer: A platform offering a variety of secure, generative AI tools designed specifically for educators to create lesson plans, worksheets, group assignments, and more.

ThingLink: A tool for creating interactive content and immersive learning experiences using AI support.

Wordwall: An educational tool used to create ESL grammar activities and adapt content for different learning levels.
