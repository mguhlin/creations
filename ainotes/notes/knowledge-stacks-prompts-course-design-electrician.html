Online Electrician Prep Curriculum (12–16 weeks, self-paced)

What you’ll be able to do by the end

  • Explain and calculate basic circuit behavior (Ohm’s law, power, series/parallel).
  • Read wiring diagrams and simple plans.
  • Choose conductors, breakers, and basic raceways using the current NEC used in your area.
  • Understand grounding/bonding fundamentals.
  • Know safe work practices, tools, and common residential/light commercial methods.

---

Week 0: Setup (1–2 days)

Get:

  • A basic electrical theory text (any reputable one)
  • NEC codebook (the edition adopted where you live)
  • A test-prep guide (Journeyman-style questions are useful even early)
  • Notebook + calculator

Optional (recommended) low-cost kit for practice at home (no live power):

  • Multimeter
  • Non-contact voltage tester (for later, in the field)
  • Wire strippers, lineman pliers, screwdrivers
  • Small breadboard + low-voltage power supply (safe circuit practice)

---

Module 1 (Weeks 1–2): Electrical Fundamentals

Topics

  • Voltage, current, resistance, power (V=IR, P=VI)
  • Series/parallel circuits
  • AC vs DC basics
  • Basic magnetism and induction
  • Multimeter basics (voltage, resistance, continuity)

Practice

  • Daily 10–15 calculation problems
  • Build low-voltage series/parallel circuits and measure values

Assessment

  • Quiz: 25 problems + explain each step (no guessing)

---

Module 2 (Weeks 3–4): Tools, Materials, and Safety

Topics

  • Hand tools, meters, torque basics
  • Wire types (THHN/THWN, NM-B, MC), insulation ratings
  • Boxes, connectors, fittings
  • Hazards: shock, arc flash, ladders, confined spaces
  • Lockout/tagout concepts, PPE, jobsite communication

Practice

  • Identify parts from images; “name + purpose” drills
  • Create a safety checklist for a typical service call

Assessment

  • Safety scenario responses (what you do, what you don’t do, why)

---

Module 3 (Weeks 5–6): Wiring Diagrams and Circuit Types

Topics

  • Symbols, ladder diagrams, one-lines (intro), schematics
  • Switch loops, 3-way/4-way switching logic
  • Receptacle circuits, lighting circuits
  • GFCI/AFCI purpose and typical placement (conceptual + code awareness)

Practice

  • Draw circuits from written descriptions
  • Diagnose “what’s wrong” from a diagram with a fault

Assessment

  • Diagram exam: interpret + sketch 6 circuits

---

Module 4 (Weeks 7–9): Code Navigation (NEC Skills)

Goal: become fast at finding rules and exceptions.

Topics

  • NEC structure: Articles, parts, tables, annexes (how to look things up)
  • Conductor ampacity and derating concepts
  • Overcurrent protection concepts
  • Box fill concept
  • Basic raceway fill concept (intro)
  • General requirements for branch circuits, receptacles, lighting outlets (overview)
  • Working space, support/secure, protection from physical damage (overview)

Practice (daily)

  • “Code scavenger hunt”: 10 lookups/day (timed)
  • 1 mixed worksheet/day (ampacity + OCPD + box fill concept questions)

Assessment

  • Open-book timed code exam (60–90 minutes)

---

Module 5 (Weeks 10–12): Grounding and Bonding + Load Concepts

Topics

  • Grounding vs bonding (clear distinction)
  • Equipment grounding conductors vs grounded conductors (neutral)
  • Fault current path concept
  • Service vs subpanel basics (conceptual)
  • Load calculations (intro to dwellings and small panels—enough to understand the logic)

Practice

  • Explain in plain language: “What happens during a fault?”
  • Do basic load and circuit sizing exercises (not advanced design)

Assessment

  • Short-answer + calculation test

---

Module 6 (Weeks 13–16, optional but strong): Applied Troubleshooting

Topics

  • “Symptom → likely causes” logic
  • Meter-based troubleshooting steps (safe, simulated when possible)
  • Common residential issues: open neutral, reversed polarity, tripped GFCI, loose connections (conceptual)

Practice

  • Case studies (written): pick the next diagnostic step and justify
  • Create your own troubleshooting flowchart

Assessment

  • 10 scenario practical (written) exam

---

Weekly cadence (works well for adults)

  • 3 days/week: reading + notes (45–60 min)
  • 2 days/week: problems + code lookups (45–60 min)
  • 1 day/week: review + timed quiz (30–45 min)

Code learning: NEC + an NEC “how to use the codebook” companion.
