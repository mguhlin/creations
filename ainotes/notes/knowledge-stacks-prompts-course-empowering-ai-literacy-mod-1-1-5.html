Slide 1: Importance of AI Literacy

Definition: AI literacy refers to understanding the capabilities, limitations, and ethical considerations of AI, enabling students to navigate and interact with AI systems effectively.

Physical Example: Teaching AI Concepts through Interactive Tools

  • Example: Platforms like Scratch or Google Teachable Machine allow students to experiment with AI concepts like machine learning and pattern recognition in a hands-on environment.
  • Explanation: By using tools like Teachable Machine, students can create their own AI models, such as teaching a machine to recognize objects through images. This interactive experience builds a foundational understanding of how AI works and helps demystify its use. Through these activities, students can develop critical thinking skills as they evaluate the strengths and limitations of the AI models they build, promoting AI literacy from an early age.

Slide 2: Strategies for Integrating AI Literacy into PreK-16 Education

Definition: Integrating AI literacy involves embedding AI-related concepts and activities into the curriculum at all grade levels to prepare students for an AI-driven world.

Physical Example: AI-Based Collaborative Learning Projects

  • Example: Students in grades 6-8 collaborate on projects where they combine different AI tools, such as using AI for data analysis and text generation to solve real-world problems, like understanding climate change.
  • Explanation: Through projects like these, students explore the capabilities of multiple AI tools and learn how to integrate them effectively. This promotes skills in collaboration, problem-solving, and innovation. Activities like creating algorithms to predict environmental changes or analyzing datasets with AI not only build technical skills but also encourage students to think critically about how AI can be applied to address global challenges. These experiences align with TCEA’s AI Literacy Framework for Students, where students "experiment" and "build" on their knowledge of AI concepts.

Slide 3: TCEA Essential Learning Expectations for AI in Education

Definition: TCEA’s framework outlines essential skills and activities that help educators implement AI literacy across K-12, ensuring that students understand AI systems and their applications.

Physical Example: Creating Machine Learning Models in the Classroom

  • Example: In a Grade 8 science class, students build a simple machine learning model to predict plant growth based on environmental factors. This activity connects directly to TEKS standards, such as Science TEKS 8.11(A), which focuses on how organisms depend on biotic and abiotic factors.
  • Explanation: This activity allows students to interact with AI in a scientific context, giving them firsthand experience in creating and analyzing models. They can see how AI is used to make predictions, evaluate the accuracy of those predictions, and understand the limitations of AI in biological systems. This aligns with the TCEA Essential Learning Expectations for AI in Education, where students "know how AI systems work and their capabilities in educational contexts."

Slide 4: AI Literacy Framework for Students

Definition: The AI Literacy Framework provides a structured approach for teaching AI across grade levels, from understanding basic AI concepts in early education to innovating with AI tools in high school.

Physical Example: AI Projects that Foster Creativity and Innovation

  • Example: High school students use AI tools to design personalized learning games or create AI-generated art pieces, leveraging their knowledge of AI for creative projects. These activities align with the "Innovate" phase of the AI Literacy Framework for Students.
  • Explanation: Through these projects, students not only learn technical skills but also explore the intersection of AI and creativity. By using AI to create new products or artistic works, they engage in higher-order thinking, innovation, and collaboration. These activities show how AI can enhance creativity, making it a valuable tool for diverse learning styles and career paths in the future.

Key Points to Remember:

  • AI literacy is essential for helping students understand both the power and limitations of AI, preparing them for future careers.
  • Integrating AI into the curriculum from PreK-16 can be done through collaborative projects, hands-on learning, and critical thinking exercises that align with students’ developmental stages.
  • TCEA's AI literacy frameworks offer essential learning expectations that educators can use to introduce AI concepts at various grade levels, ensuring students are ready to interact with AI effectively.
