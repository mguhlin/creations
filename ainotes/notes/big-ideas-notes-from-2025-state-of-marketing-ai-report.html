Notes from 2025 State of Marketing AI Report

From Paul Roetzer

  • Every company leader faced two clear paths: Either commit to AI literacy for all in order to build a competitive advantage with AI, or become obsolete.
  • Based on working with dozens of executives on AI transformation this year alone is that we’ve already been seeing “quiet AI layoffs” for the past 6-12 months. This is where companies replace staff with AI or, at minimum, refrain from hiring new staff due to AI. In either event, they have masked these moves under things like return-to-work policies to avoid bad PR.
  • While AI adoption and literacy are at

all-time highs, so are the levels of pessimism and

uncertainty about AI’s impact on jobs.

  • The people who choose to embrace AI, experiment with it, and continually expand their understanding of it have the greatest opportunity to thrive moving forward.

The easiest way to do that is by working for a company that takes a responsible, human-centered approach to scaling AI throughout the organization.

Executive Summary

Marketers are racing ahead with AI

  • Transformation: 17% are actively reimagining their jobs with AI
  • Integration: 26% are embedding AI into workflows and processes
  • Experimentation: 40% of respondents are experimenting with AI
  • Curious: 17% are just in the early stages of understanding AI

Saving Time

  • 82% said that reducing

the time they spend on repetitive, data-driven tasks was the primary outcome they’re trying to achieve with AI.

  • 37% says efficiency/time saving is what most excites them about AI

Favorite AI Tool

  • 57% prefer ChatGPT
  • Company provided ChatGPT accounts accounted for 61% of respondents over Copilot, Google Gemini, etc.
  • As companies get bigger, ChatGPT steadily declines in popularity as an answer. It peaks at firms with $1 - $10M in annual revenue, where 70% of respondents say they’re provided with a ChatGPT license.
  • Marketers once again cite a lack of education and training as the top barrier they face to AI adoption, as has been the case every single year we’ve published this report.

This year, 62% said this was a top barrier to AI adoption in their marketing, followed by a lack of awareness or understanding within their organization (52%). Sixty-eight percent of respondents also functionally have

no AI training being provided by their companies, with 44% saying they don’t have it, 21% saying it’s in development,

and 3% unsure if it exists
