Scenarios and Assessment

Scenario A: Keeping Confidence

Your colleague, Sarah, confides in you about her struggles with anxiety, which have been affecting her work performance. She asks you to keep this information private.

Q1: How do you respond to Sarah's disclosure?

a) Offer support and assure her of confidentiality

b) Suggest she speaks to the principal about it

c) Tell her you'll think about how to help

If a) is selected:

Q2: How do you maintain confidentiality?

a) Avoid discussing Sarah's situation with anyone else

b) Only mention it to close mutual colleagues who might help

c) Inform the principal discreetly to ensure proper support

If b) or c) is selected for Q1:

Q2: Why do you think this approach is best?

a) It's important for management to be aware of such issues

b) You're not equipped to handle this kind of situation alone

c) Sharing the information could lead to better support for Sarah

Scenario B: Public Recognition

You notice that your colleague, Mark, has developed an innovative teaching method that has significantly improved student engagement in his class.

Q1: How do you acknowledge Mark's achievement?

a) Privately compliment him on his innovation

b) Mention it casually to a few other teachers

c) Publicly praise him during the next staff meeting

If c) is selected:

Q2: How do you frame your public praise?

a) Focus on the specific innovation and its impact on students

b) Highlight Mark's overall excellence as a teacher

c) Mention how Mark's method has inspired you to improve your own teaching

If a) or b) is selected for Q1:

Q2: Why do you prefer this approach over public recognition?

a) To avoid making other teachers feel inadequate

b) Because Mark might be uncomfortable with public praise

c) To maintain a competitive edge among colleagues

Scenario C: Following Through

Your colleague, Lisa, asks for your help in implementing a new technology platform in her classroom. You agree to assist her after school next week.

Q1: How do you approach this commitment?

a) Schedule a specific day and time to help Lisa

b) Tell Lisa you'll drop by when you have a free moment

c) Offer to send her some online resources instead

If a) is selected:

Q2: What do you do to ensure you follow through?

a) Set a reminder and prepare materials in advance

b) Inform Lisa you might need to reschedule if something comes up

c) Ask another tech-savvy colleague to join you

If b) or c) is selected for Q1:

Q2: Why do you think this approach is preferable?

a) It allows for more flexibility in your schedule

b) Lisa should take more initiative in learning the platform

c) You're not confident in your ability to help effectively

Universal Rubric

  1. Confidentiality and Trust (0-3 points)
    • 0: Breaches confidentiality or trust
    • 1: Maintains basic confidentiality but may share some information
    • 2: Keeps information confidential and respects trust
    • 3: Actively protects confidentiality and reinforces trust
  1. Respect and Recognition (0-3 points)
    • 0: Fails to acknowledge or diminishes others' achievements
    • 1: Provides minimal or private recognition
    • 2: Offers appropriate recognition
    • 3: Provides thoughtful, specific public recognition that boosts morale
  1. Reliability and Follow-through (0-3 points)
    • 0: Fails to follow through on commitments
    • 1: Follows through inconsistently or with reminders
    • 2: Reliably follows through on commitments
    • 3: Goes above and beyond in fulfilling commitments and supporting colleagues
  1. Empathy and Support (0-3 points)
    • 0: Shows lack of empathy or support
    • 1: Offers minimal or superficial support
    • 2: Demonstrates empathy and provides appropriate support
    • 3: Exhibits strong empathy and offers comprehensive, tailored support
  1. Professional Boundaries (0-3 points)
    • 0: Crosses professional boundaries inappropriately
    • 1: Maintains basic professional boundaries with some lapses
    • 2: Consistently maintains appropriate professional boundaries
    • 3: Skillfully navigates complex situations while maintaining professional boundaries

Total possible score: 15 points

Expanded Scenarios

Scenario A: The Confidential Struggle

It's a crisp autumn morning, and you're in the teacher's lounge preparing for your classes. Sarah, a usually cheerful English teacher, approaches you with a troubled expression. She hesitates before speaking, her voice barely above a whisper.

"Can I talk to you for a moment? In private?"

You nod, concerned, and follow her to a quiet corner. Sarah reveals that she's been struggling with anxiety, which has been affecting her work. She explains how she's been having panic attacks before staff meetings and has trouble focusing during parent-teacher conferences. Her voice trembles as she confides that she's afraid of losing her job if anyone finds out.

"I don't know what to do," Sarah says, her eyes glistening. "I haven't told anyone else. Please, can you keep this between us?"

Scenario B: The Innovative Educator

During a routine classroom observation, you witness something extraordinary in Mark's history class. Instead of the usual lecture, Mark has transformed his classroom into a living museum. Students, dressed in period costumes, are enthusiastically presenting different aspects of the Industrial Revolution to their classmates.

You're impressed by the level of engagement and the depth of understanding demonstrated by the students. After class, you overhear several students excitedly discussing the lesson in the hallway.

At the next staff meeting, you notice Mark sitting quietly in the back, not drawing attention to his innovative teaching method. The principal asks if anyone has any updates or successes to share.

Scenario C: The Technology Challenge

Lisa, a veteran science teacher, approaches you after a staff meeting about implementing a new online lab simulation platform. She looks flustered and admits she's struggling with the technology.

"I know these simulations could really help my students, especially those who can't attend in-person labs," Lisa explains. "But I'm completely lost. Could you possibly help me set it up?"

You agree to assist her, knowing your schedule is already packed with grading and lesson planning. Lisa's face lights up with relief.

"Thank you so much! Could we maybe meet next week after school?" she asks hopefully.

Scenario D: The Lunchroom Incident

It's lunchtime, and you're on cafeteria duty. Suddenly, you notice a heated argument between two of your colleagues, John and Maria. Their voices are rising, and students are starting to stare.

As you approach, you hear John accuse Maria of "always hogging the good field trip slots" and Maria retorting that John "never volunteers for anything anyway."

Both teachers fall silent as they notice you. The tension is palpable, and several students are now openly watching the scene unfold.

Scenario E: The Budget Cut Dilemma

You're part of the school's budget committee, and you've just received some troubling news. Due to district-wide cuts, your school needs to reduce spending by 10% for the upcoming year. In a confidential meeting, the principal reveals that this will likely mean cutting one teaching position.

As you leave the meeting, you run into your close friend and colleague, Rachel. She's excited about some new projects she's planning for next year and asks you how the budget meeting went.

"I hope we're getting some extra funds for the art department," Rachel says enthusiastically. "I've got some great ideas for expanding our program!"

You know that Rachel's position is one of those being considered for the cut, but the information is supposed to remain confidential until the final decision is made.
