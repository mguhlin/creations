Which AI tools are teachers RECOMMENDING and which tools are students actually USING?  RAND has some fantastic survey data they haven't written up yet.

In one question, they asked students, "What AI tools, if any, do you use in class or for homework?"

Then they asked, "Which of the following tools, if any, did a teacher suggest or assign you to use?"

I plotted both those answers against each other and—whoops, would you look at that.

OpenAI has ChatGPT and Study Mode.

Google has Gemini and Guided Learning.

Anthropic has Claude and Learning Mode.

Khan Academy has Khanmigo.

Everyone has their "Socratic AI"—their AI that is somewhat less helpful than consumer-grade AI.  But students have seemingly said, in one voice, "No, thank you, I prefer the AI that gives me the answers."

Deep learning is difficult. It means unlearning and re-learning, integrating old assumptions with new data, pulling up your neurons at the root and re-planting them. It is easier than ever to get the right answer to a question without doing that very hard work.

If you want to make a dent AT ALL in student learning, you have to have a theory of how you'll help students do that work.

The dominant theory right now—just give students a weaker version of a stronger tool they can easily access at a different URL; just put the cookies on a slightly higher shelf than the vegetables—is clearly inadequate.
