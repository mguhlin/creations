Generative AI Adoption Checklist for NSPA Member Organizations

Use this checklist to guide scholarship provider organizations from top-down permission to bottom-up momentum. The focus is practical, mission-aligned adoption that protects students, staff, and trust.

---

1. Legitimize Use Rather Than Police It

☐ Publish a clear, accessible AI use policy for staff and volunteers

☐ Frame guidance as enablement, not restriction

☐ Acknowledge that staff already use AI tools outside work and channel that fluency responsibly

☐ Replace fear of compliance with curiosity and structured experimentation

☐ Reinforce that thoughtful progress matters more than perfection

Key question: Do your policies reduce hesitation or create it?

---

2. Start With High-Impact Scholarship Wins

☐ Identify one visible, achievable AI pilot for this application cycle or quarter

☐ Prioritize time-saving use cases such as drafting communications, summarizing materials, or organizing data

☐ Document what worked and what did not

☐ Share early lessons learned across teams, not just successes

☐ Build confidence before expanding into higher-risk workflows

Key question: What small win would convert skepticism into trust?

---

3. Protect What Matters Most: Students and Data

☐ Define non-negotiable boundaries around student and applicant data

☐ Establish clear rules for what information may and may not be shared with AI tools

☐ Vet AI vendors for security practices, data retention, and compliance standards

☐ Create a simple, risk-based approval process for new tools

☐ Train staff on what must never be entered into an AI system

Key question: Have you clearly defined your “do not cross” lines?

---

4. Upskill Staff Rather Than Replace Judgment

☐ Clearly communicate that AI supports staff work rather than replacing roles

☐ Provide role-relevant professional learning for reviewers, communications staff, and leadership

☐ Invite staff to identify tasks where AI could responsibly reduce friction

☐ Encourage peer mentoring from early adopters

☐ Recognize staff who develop safer, more effective workflows

Key question: Are you investing in people at the same pace as tools?

---

5. Anchor AI Use to Scholarship Mission and Values

☐ Explicitly connect AI initiatives to equity, access, and student support

☐ Ensure AI use strengthens rather than weakens fairness and trust

☐ Preserve human judgment in applicant-facing and donor-facing decisions

☐ Regularly ask whether AI use enhances or erodes your organization’s “human touch”

☐ Ground strategic decisions in purpose, not novelty

Key question: Does this use of AI advance your mission or distract from it?

---

6. Create an Internal AI Advisory Lens

☐ Use AI to model different stakeholder perspectives before board or leadership discussions

☐ Pressure-test ideas against more cautious and more innovative viewpoints

☐ Include equity-focused, compliance-focused, and operational perspectives

☐ Draw from existing personas such as applicants, reviewers, donors, and partners

☐ Use this advisory lens for scenario planning, not decision replacement

☐ Ensure all experimentation occurs in secure, approved environments

Key question: Who challenges your assumptions, and how often do you hear them?

---
