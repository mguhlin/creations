Trust is the residue from promises kept.

Divide things into conversations that really lead to alignment.

We have access to a lot of knowledge and information. Having access to that info isn't enough. We also have shared agreements about how that information is going to impact our own world, and organization.

Volume=value. We're storing too much information in digital repositories, but we don't actually look at them over time. We're turning into digital hoarders. In terms of knowledge stack, volume becomes a problem because the AI can't operationalize all of them, to apply them to a situation. It doesn't hurt to organize the knowledge stack to make it easier for the AI to retrieve information. It also makes it easier to replace information. The best example is the high-effect size instructional strategies. Those effect sizes vary from year to year and have to be updated. If I include those effect size everywhere in my knowledge stack, it's hard to update all of them. Instead, I can create modules of information, popping the modules in and out, replacing them with current info, makes it easier to update them.

Start out with what the client values...what are the experiences the client wants to have?

Based on those desires, how can we develop a standard of quality that aligns to those?

Once we have those, we can start making decisions. We can determine how good those decisions are based on how well they align to those standards.

Uncertainties and risk are the next thing to be concerned about since they interfere with the quality of the decisions we make.
