S3: Enterprise AI is Held Back by Issues

  • Data privacy and IP concerns: AI models can access confidential company info you enter into it. Training models on other people's illegaly gained intellectual property.
  • Hallucinations: AI provides incorrect answers confidently when pushed outside its limits. Sometimes make stuff up. Don't want to put bad information in front of customers.
  • Bias: AI was trained on humans, so can bring biases into its answers that are sometimes hard to detect. This is fading as tech is working actively to remove it.

  • A good step for the industry
  • Teams can buy their way out
  • ChatGPT for Teams - Does not train the model on your data ($25/month with minimum of 2 seats)

  • Consumers can turn off training the model on your data (free or $20/month for GPT Plus). I get more benefit from these models than they do from my data

S6: Open AI's Approach to Data Privacy

Data Usage Policy

  • ChatGPT Team, Enterprise, and API data not used for model training
  • API inputs/outputs securely retained for up to 30 days for service provision and abuse detection

Data Retention

  • API data removed after 30 days unless legally required to retain
  • User history may be stored for user access

Content Monitoring

  • Reviews conducted for illicit use, disinformation, or explicit content
  • Violation may result in platform removal

S12: But It Gets Complicated

  1. Beyond public data

To make the LLM useful, enterprises need to be able to share sensitive information (not just train on public data)

  1. User interaction privacy

The more you share, the better the AI becomes ... but now you're sharing more

  1. Different levels of security

Depending on the implementation of the AI and your other systems (e.g., Apple Intelligence)

<mark>All this means...deployments are stalling because of real cybersecurity concerns.</mark>

  • Safer is if data is on device (smartphone), rather than sent to the cloud

S14: What's Really Needed for Enterprise Success

  • CEO talk about AI backup with funded AI plans. Some do it to sound innovative, others because they see it saving money. But neither matches their talk with action and funding.These moments require leadership. It's not easy to integrate, but this is a change management and understanding what workflows will benefit from AI.
  • Heads of GenAI that understand the business needs and workflows
  • Lower workforce anxiety, higher AI proficiency.
  • Carefully chosen AI pilots for early wins/lessons. You need a head of AI that represent uses cases across the organization, where employees will embrace AI in that context.
  • Metrics and benchmarking. Start with anecdotal evidence, and then track other metrics over time.

Key Findings

  • Only 7% of professionals are in the "AI class" - those who have integrated AI into their personal workflows
  • Nearly 60% are "AI Newcomers" who have only used ChatGPT a few times
  • Members of the AI class save up to 12 hours of working time per week using AI
  • Most companies won't see efficiency gains with just a small AI task force - they need to provide more employees with AI guidelines, approved tools, and training
