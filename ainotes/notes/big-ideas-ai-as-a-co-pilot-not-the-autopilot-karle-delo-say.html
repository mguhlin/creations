📝 Why AI Feedback in Education Needs a Reality Check

AI can be a powerful tool for personalized feedback, but we need to take a careful approach when guiding students. As someone who frequently uses AI for feedback on my own work, I know its benefits and its blind spots.

A Cautionary Tale:

Whenever I finish a talk, I record it, get a transcript, and ask ChatGPT for feedback. Although it provides useful constructive criticism, it consistently suggests removing sections I know resonate the most with my audience. These are moments people remember, bring up in conversations, and highlight in feedback surveys. But the only reason I can identify this disconnect? Years of experience and collecting multiple forms of feedback.

Impact on Students:

Imagine a student following AI’s advice to cut out the “best part” of their writing simply because they’re still developing that critical judgment. This is why it’s essential to teach students that AI should never be their only feedback source. Encourage them to combine AI insights with feedback from peers, teachers, or their own observations.

Empowering students to say no to AI feedback is a vital part of this process. It’s about building their confidence in trusting their own judgment, even if AI suggests otherwise. This way, students learn to see AI as a co-pilot—not the autopilot.
