A formula for blog writing that aligns with the FLOATER framework, ensuring high scores for credibility and quality, can be structured as follows:

  1. Start with a Clear, Testable Claim (Falsifiability)

• Goal: Define the main point or claim of the blog in a way that can be tested or evaluated.

• Tips:

• Use specific, measurable language.

• Avoid vague or overly broad statements.

• Example: “This AI tool improves critical thinking skills by 40% based on user data.”

  1. Build with Sound Logic

• Goal: Ensure the argument flows logically and is supported by well-reasoned steps.

• Tips:

• Present a clear thesis statement early on.

• Use a structure like Problem → Solution → Evidence → Benefits.

• Avoid logical fallacies such as strawman arguments or false dilemmas.

  1. Incorporate Objectivity

• Goal: Maintain balance by addressing multiple perspectives.

• Tips:

• Acknowledge opposing viewpoints and respond respectfully.

• Disclose any biases or affiliations that may influence the perspective.

• Example: “While some critics argue that AI tools oversimplify critical thinking, studies suggest otherwise.”

  1. Consider Alternative Explanations

• Goal: Explore other plausible reasons or methods related to the topic.

• Tips:

• Present and compare alternatives to your claim or solution.

• Example: “Other frameworks like the Socratic Method have also been used to teach critical thinking, but AI tools may offer scalability.”

  1. Use Tentative Language for Conclusions

• Goal: Present conclusions as subject to revision based on future evidence.

• Tips:

• Use phrases like “the evidence suggests” or “current data points to.”

• Avoid absolute claims unless there is overwhelming evidence.

• Example: “This AI tool appears effective in specific contexts but requires further testing in diverse settings.”

  1. Back Up Claims with Robust Evidence

• Goal: Provide reliable and sufficient evidence to support claims.

• Tips:

• Use peer-reviewed research, reputable statistics, or expert opinions.

• Include hyperlinks to credible sources for transparency.

• Example: “According to a 2025 study in Educational Psychology Review, AI-assisted learning tools increased critical thinking scores by 30%.”

  1. Ensure Replicability

• Goal: Offer clear methodologies or frameworks readers can verify or apply.

• Tips:

• Share specific steps or data collection methods.

• Provide enough detail for others to replicate the results.

• Example: “This study used a randomized control trial with 200 participants over 12 weeks.”

Bonus Tips for Engagement:

  1. Use a Compelling Hook: Start with an intriguing question or anecdote to draw readers in.
  1. Incorporate Visuals: Use charts, infographics, or videos to clarify complex points.
  1. Conclude with a Call to Action: Encourage readers to engage, share, or apply the information.

Example Blog Outline Using the Formula

  1. Title: “Can AI Really Teach Critical Thinking? Here’s What the Data Says”
  1. Introduction:

• Hook: A surprising statistic or question about critical thinking skills.

• Claim: AI tools can enhance critical thinking by measurable margins.

  1. Body:

• Logical breakdown of how the AI works.

• Evidence from studies or user data.

• Alternative frameworks discussed (e.g., manual vs. automated teaching methods).

• Acknowledgment of limitations and areas for improvement.

  1. Conclusion:

• Tentative summary of findings.

• Call to action: “Test this tool and share your feedback.”

By following this formula, writers can produce balanced, credible, and engaging content that scores high on the FLOATER scale and resonates with readers.
