Course Module Overview

Module 1: Foundations of AI in Education with BoodleBox

In this module, you will learn the fundamentals of BoodleBox Unlimited. You will explore the user interface, understand the value of using multiple premium AI models, and practice writing effective prompts to get started. This foundational knowledge will set you up for success in integrating AI into your daily teaching practice.

Module 2: AI-Powered Lesson Planning and Content Creation

In this module, you will dive into practical applications for your own workload. You will use BoodleBox to brainstorm ideas, generate and rewrite lesson plans to meet specific standards, differentiate instructional materials for diverse learners, and create engaging content like exit tickets and review questions, saving you valuable time.

Module 3: Engaging Students and Streamlining Assessment with AI

In this module, you will shift your focus to student-facing activities. You will learn how to design AI-enhanced assignments that promote critical thinking, use AI to help create fair and consistent rubrics, and explore methods for evaluating both the final student product and their AI interaction process.

Module 4: Advanced BoodleBox: Collaboration and Customization

In this module, you will explore the advanced features of BoodleBox Unlimited. You will learn how to collaborate with colleagues in shared chats, stack multiple bots for complex problem-solving, and build your own custom BoodleBot tailored to a specific subject or classroom need, enhancing your productivity and professional learning.

Module 5: Leading with AI: Ethics, Safety, and School-Wide Integration

In this module, you will examine the bigger picture of AI in your school or district. You will learn about responsible AI implementation, data privacy compliance with FERPA and SOC 2, and strategies for monitoring student use. You will also explore how to advocate for and support a school-wide AI initiative.

1.1.3 - Writing Your First Effective Prompt

This video will guide you through the basics of prompt engineering, helping you write clear instructions to get the best results from the AI.

[mod113.mp4]

Script: _The quality of the AI's response depends entirely on the quality of your instruction, or "prompt." Let's learn a simple formula for success: Role, Task, Context, and Format. First, tell the AI its Role, such as "Act as a high school history teacher." Next, state the specific Task: "Create five multiple-choice questions." Then, provide Context: "The questions should be about the causes of the American Revolution." Finally, specify the Format: "Provide the question, four answer choices, and indicate the correct answer." By using this structure, you move from a vague request to a precise instruction that gives you exactly what you need._

For example, you could ask, "@MySyllabus.pdf, what is your late work policy?" The AI will read your document and provide an answer based only on the information within it. This is incredibly powerful for working with your specific curriculum and classroom materials._

Problem

You are teaching a unit on poetry to your eighth-grade English class. You want to introduce the concept of "metaphor" but know that a dry definition will not be engaging. You need a creative lesson hook, a clear and simple definition of the term, and three examples of metaphors from popular songs that your students might recognize. How would you use BoodleBox to generate these three distinct elements for your lesson?

Solution

[mod221.mp4]

This video demonstrates how to use a single, well-structured prompt to generate multiple, distinct creative components for a single lesson.

Script: _Let's tackle this common lesson planning challenge. We need three things: a hook, a definition, and examples. We can get all of this in one prompt. Here is what we will write: "Act as a highly engaging eighth-grade English teacher who loves music. I need help creating a lesson introduction for the literary device 'metaphor.' Please provide the following three things in your response. First, write a creative and surprising lesson hook to grab student attention. Second, provide a clear, simple definition of 'metaphor' that an eighth grader can easily understand. Third, provide three examples of a metaphor from popular songs from the last ten years, and briefly explain what two things are being compared in each example. Please label each of the three sections clearly." As you can see, the AI has perfectly followed our instructions, giving us three distinct, ready-to-use components for our lesson. This multi-part prompting is a powerful way to build out a lesson quickly._
