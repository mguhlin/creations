What strategy do you teach your students to help them read & comprehend word problems?

Assistant

To help students read and comprehend word problems effectively, strategies like CUBES, SOLVE, or 3-Read are valuable approaches. Here’s a breakdown of these and related evidence-based methods:

Strategies to Teach Word Problem Comprehension:
  1. CUBES (Circle, Underline, Box, Evaluate, Solve):
    • Circle important numbers.
    • Underline the question.
    • Box key math terms.
    • Evaluate what operations or steps are needed.
    • Solve and check the work.
  1. 3-Read Strategy:
    • First Read: Understand the context (what is happening in the problem).
    • Second Read: Identify the question and key details.
    • Third Read: Determine the operations or strategies required to solve it.
  1. SOLVE Strategy:
    • State the problem.
    • Organize the information.
    • Line up a plan.
    • Verify the steps.
    • Evaluate the solution.
Supporting Comprehension with Evidence-Based Practices:
  • Summarization (Effect Size: 0.74): Teach students to summarize the problem in their own words to reinforce understanding.
  • Reciprocal Teaching (Effect Size: 0.86): Guide students through predicting, questioning, clarifying, and summarizing steps within a group setting.
  • Think-Pair-Share: Encourage collaborative discussions for clarifying and problem-solving.

Here’s an age-appropriate model acronym for grades 3–10 that captures evidence-based processes: STRIDE

STRIDE Model

S – Scan the Problem:

  • Look for important numbers, keywords, and questions.
    • Use a strategy like circling or underlining to organize information visually.

T – Think About Connections:

  • Connect the problem to what you already know.
    • Visualize or draw the situation to build understanding.

R – Reflect on the Question:

  • Ask yourself: What is the problem asking me to find or do?
    • Rephrase the question in your own words for clarity.

I – Identify a Strategy:

  • Decide what steps or operations will help solve the problem (e.g., addition, subtraction, or creating a diagram).
    • Use tools like graphic organizers or charts if needed.

D – Do the Work:

  • Solve step by step, checking each calculation or action.
    • Write down your reasoning to track your thinking process.

E – Evaluate Your Answer:

  • Check: Does the answer make sense in the context of the problem?
    • If something seems off, review the steps or try a different approach.
