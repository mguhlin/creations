AI Prompt for Revising Writing

Version 1

Objective:

Revise the given piece of writing to ensure it is clear, concise, engaging, and effective by following the combined principles of Hemingway, Vonnegut, and clear writing guidelines.

Instructions:

1. Outline the Main Points

  • Plan Ahead: Create an outline of the main points. Use a list or digital note system to jot down one point per note.
  • Organize Logically: Arrange these points in a logical order to ensure the writing flows naturally and stays on track.

2. Start Where the Readers Are

  • Know Your Audience: Consider what the readers already know about the topic. Avoid talking down to them but make sure the writing is accessible.
  • Engage Directly: Write in a way that connects with the readers' current level of understanding.

3. Avoid Jargon

  • Use Simple Words: Choose familiar, everyday words over technical jargon. This makes the writing more relatable and easier to understand.
  • Explain When Necessary: If a technical term must be used, explain it in simple language.

4. Use Familiar Combinations of Words

  • Common Phrases: Opt for common phrases instead of complex ones. For example, say "protecting students' personal information" instead of "safeguarding sensitive student data."
  • Clarity Over Complexity: Prioritize clarity and simplicity in word choices.

5. Use First-Degree Words

  • Direct Language: Use simple, direct words that immediately convey the meaning. For instance, "use strong security" instead of "implement robust security measures."
  • Avoid Overly Formal Words: Stick to straightforward language that anyone can understand.

6. Stick to the Point

  • Cut the Fluff: Eliminate unnecessary details and anecdotes. Focus on the main points to keep the writing concise.
  • One Fact Per Sentence: Usually, one fact or example per sentence is enough to support the point.

7. Be as Brief as Possible

  • Condense Writing: Shorten and tighten sentences to make them clearer and more impactful.
  • Logical Order: Present points in a logical ABC order. Avoid over-explaining and keep it straightforward.

8. Look for Common Word Wasters

  • Windy Phrases: Replace long-winded phrases with shorter alternatives. For example, "due to the fact that" can be shortened to "because."
  • Passive Voice: Convert passive sentences to active ones. For example, "The test was completed by the student" becomes "The student completed the test."

9. Embrace Vonnegut's Style

  • Be Kind: Write with empathy and understanding. Imagine explaining something to a friend.
  • Humor and Humanity: Inject a bit of humor and humanity into the writing to make it more engaging.

10. Embrace Hemingway's Style

  • Short Sentences: Use short, punchy sentences. Hemingway believed in brevity and clarity.
  • Simple Structure: Keep sentence structures simple. Avoid complex, multi-clause sentences.

11. Revise Ruthlessly

  • Edit for Clarity: After writing, review the work to ensure every sentence is clear and necessary.
  • Feedback Loop: Get feedback from others to identify areas that need improvement.

12. Cut Out Excess Evidence and Unnecessary Anecdotes

  • Focus on Facts: Usually, one fact or example per sentence is enough to support a point.
  • Avoid Unnecessary Stories: If an anecdote doesn't add value, remove it.

13. Look for Positive/Negative Sections

  • Cut the Negative: Focus on positive phrasing where possible. For example, instead of saying "do not use carelessness," say "use caution."

14. Write Clearly

  • Say it in Fewer Words: When finished, strive to say the same thing in fewer words.

By following these guidelines, revise the given piece of writing to enhance its clarity, conciseness, and engagement, ensuring it effectively communicates the intended message.
