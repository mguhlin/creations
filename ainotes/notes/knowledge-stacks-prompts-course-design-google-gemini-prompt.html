Adhere to the TCEA Style Guide below

AVOID the following:

  • Ampersand (&) use; instead use “and”
  • Placement of two spaces after a period at the end of a sentence
  • Overuse of exclamation marks (!)
  • Typing the full hyperlink and then linking it (https://tcea.org )
  • Putting periods at the ends of bulleted or numbered lists unless they are complete sentences

DO the following

  • Use the Oxford comma
  • Capitalize if the product being taught is normally capitalized
  • Hyperlink text of the link rather than adding the link itself (TCEA Courses)
  • Bold links
  • Explain common abbreviations like LMS, IEP, etc.
  • Write out numbers up to ten (“Take three of these….” instead of “Take 3 of these…”)
  • Capitalize the first letter of each bullet or numbered list item
  • If a punctuation mark is needed to separate items surrounded by quotation marks, put it inside the quotation marks. (Save the file and click "Open." instead of Save the File and click "Open".)
  • Rely on second person (e.g. “you” perspective and “you, your, yours, yourself, yourselves”)

Overall Course Organization

Each Module has the following sections and I want you to organize the course you generate into similar sections with sequential numbering. Here's structure for Module 1:

1.0 About - This section quotes a relevant education text about the topic, and a brief paragraph with a zippy "Let's go!' suggestion. Make sure to use only quotes from real people and included embedded link in parenthetical (source)

1.1 Explore - This section that includes a brief paragraph introduction, a list of Capacity Matrix indicators for that module, a Video section with 3-5 videos (1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.1.3, 1.1.4, 1.1.5) with a one sentence description what that video should be about. Videos mostly focus on how-to with a few key concepts in a slide deck.

1.2 Resources - this section includes five embedded links (link the titles of the resources) to a variety of media, such as videos, blog entries, research articles, and blog entries from https://blog.tcea.org that are relevant and working. Please only pull working links for the resources section of each, don't make them up. Also, include more than simply TCEA blog links. Include videos and diagram links, too.

1.3 Practice On Your Own - a section focused on presenting a real-life problem-based learning scenario with a solution video by the course developer. The scenario should capture application of the knowledge and ideas shared in the module.

1.4 Fast Fact Check - a quiz section that mixes questions from modules in sequential order as each module is completed. Given the content in Modules 1-5, please come up with short 5 question quizzes (multiple choice, True/False) that employ retrieval practice and interleaving instructional strategies. Organize results below by Module section, and indicate the correct answer for each quiz. For example:

  • Module 1 would only have questions from Module 1 in it
  • Module 2 would have questions from Module 1 and Module 2 in it (but no more than 5)
  • Module 3 would have questions from Module 1, 2, and 3 in it (but no more than 5)
  • Module 4 would have questions from Module 1,2, and 3 in it (but no more than 5)

Indicate what module the question came from after the question as a parenthetical (Module 1)

Finally, preface each quiz with some variant of instructions like these:

  • Quick check! Answer easy questions on recent topics, with some earlier material mixed in to boost your recall.
  • Ready for a mini-review? Tackle simple questions about what you've just learned, plus a few from past lessons.
  • Brief quiz time! Test your grasp of new content with multiple-choice and true/false questions, sprinkled with earlier topics for better retention.

1.5 Vignette Review

Come up with a short vignette written for teachers and/or school administrators that captures core ideas of each module that can be used as a reflection piece. Include a short reflection question for each vignette. Some suggestions for the type of vignette reflection question are included below:

  • What is your first reaction?
  • How are you feeling about what is happening in the vignette?
  • Do you feel conflicted with what the characters in the vignette are doing? If yes, why. If not, why not?
  • What issues would some staff have with what is happening in this situation (that are different than your own)?
  • Have you ever worked with a colleague that had a different understanding of the centre policies? If you have, what was the policy and how did you handle the situation?
  • When you disagree with one of your co-workers decisions, do you voice your opinion? If not, why not?

  • What are the questions that come to mind when you watch this situation?
  • Does your centre have rules or policies about this situation? If the centre does, why do you agree or disagree? If the centre doesn’t, should there be a policy/rule? What should this be and why?
  • Now, what do you feel is the best way to handle this situation?

Teachers have turned to screen recording and casting to facilitate online instruction. About Module 1

Creating accessible learning can be challenging. One of the ways that you can do that is to be more aware of how children interact with your digital creations. For example, is your content accessible via text, audio, and video or images? Jennifer Snelling shares ways to make remote learning accessible to students.

Make Remote Learning Accessible

You can read Jennifer’s article Seven Ways To Make Remote Learning Accessible to All Students online (available online on September 11, 2020), but here are some of the main points:

  • Give students the option of reading text, watching videos, listening to audio, or examining images.
  • Heighten student engagement with hands-on or project-based activities.
  • Design open-ended activities where students choose how to demonstrate their knowledge. This can include audio/video podcasts or creating a picture or drawing.
  • Add closed captions to videos.

In this module, we are going to explore some ways to accomplish these points. Ready to begin?

1.1 Accessibility

In this module, you will learn several valuable skills to make remote learning more accessible. One that you can start with easily is closed captioning. What’s more, tools like Microsoft’s Immersive Reader and Chrome browser add-ons make it so much easier. In these videos, you will see ways to use digital tools to achieve what Snell recommends in her article.

Videos

In these videos, you will learn how to actualize the capacity matrix in your own teaching efforts.

1.1.1 - Adding Closed Captioning to Google Slides

[mod111.mp4]

“Closed captioning in Slides can help audience members … who are deaf or hard of hearing, but it can also be useful for audience members without hearing loss who are listening in noisy auditoriums or rooms with poor sound settings,” G Suite Accessibility Software Engineers Laura D’Aquila and Abigail Klein wrote in a blog post recently. “Closed captioning can also be a benefit when the presenter is speaking a non-native language or is not projecting their voice.” Learn more.

1.1.2 - Adding Closed Captioning to Microsoft PowerPoint

[mod112.mp4]

In PowerPoint for Windows, you can add closed captions or subtitles to videos and audio files in your presentations. Adding closed captions makes your presentation accessible to a larger audience, including people with hearing disabilities and those who speak languages other than the one in your video. Learn more.

1.1.3 - Use Flipgrid to Generate Audio Transcriptions from Audio and Video Clips That are 10 Minutes or Less

[mod113.mp4]

Flipgrid offers storage of up to 10-minute videos. What many may not realize is that it will also convert audio/video files you upload and, in time, offer you closed captioning (a.k.a. audio transcription) for those files.

1.1.4 - See How Microsoft Immersive Reader Enhances the Student Experience in Web Browsers and Tools Like Wakelet and Flipgrid

[mod114.mp4]

Immersive Reader includes support for emerging readers through features like Line Focus and Picture Dictionary. Its support is included in all Microsoft tools, such as OneNote. Learn more.

1.1.5 - Explore Microsoft Translator Website and App, as Well as Google Translate Website

[mod115.mp4]

“Microsoft Translator live is a free translation and transcription service that lets you have translated conversations across multiple devices on iOS, Android, and more” (source: Microsoft Translator). Google Translate also boasts a website and app you can use.
