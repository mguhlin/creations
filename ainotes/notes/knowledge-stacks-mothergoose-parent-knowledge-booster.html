Additional Formatting Requirements

  • Time Management: Ensure total time across all parts equals 30 minutes (typically three 10-minute parts: 6 min video + 2 min hands-on + 2 min reflection).
  • Parent-Centered Language: Throughout all materials, use warm, accessible, conversational language that speaks directly to parents. Avoid educational jargon, acronyms, or technical terms without explanation. Focus on practical benefits and real-world application at home.
  • Dual-Benefit Focus: Every activity, resource, and strategy should clearly show how it benefits both the parent (learning a skill) and the child (receiving better support). Make this connection explicit.
  • Consistency: Maintain consistent terminology, frameworks, and tone throughout all three parts. If you introduce a concept or tool in Part 1, reference it naturally in Parts 2 and 3 to build continuity.
  • Progressive Complexity: Structure content so:
    • Part 1 introduces foundational concepts and builds confidence with simple, achievable actions
      • Part 2 provides hands-on practice and deeper exploration with more complex applications
      • Part 3 focuses on implementation, troubleshooting common challenges, and long-term success strategies
  • Accessibility Considerations: Ensure all recommended tools, resources, and activities are:
    • Free or low-cost (acknowledge budget constraints)
      • Accessible on multiple devices (computer, tablet, smartphone)
      • Available in Spanish when possible (note this in materials)
      • Appropriate for varying levels of tech comfort and digital literacy
      • Inclusive of diverse family structures and situations
  • Include at the end: Add a "Keep Learning & Growing" section with:
    • 3 recommended next Parent Knowledge Boosters that build on this topic
      • 1-2 free resources for continued learning (websites, apps, community resources)
      • An encouraging message about the parent's role in their child's education
