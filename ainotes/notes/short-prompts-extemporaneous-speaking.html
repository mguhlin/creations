"My favorite event was extemporaneous speaking. We walked in a room, got a question, had 30 minutes to research and write a speech. We used magazines and built a database on how to find info. Teaching a class on debate with a focus on extemporaneous speaking, based on the provided description, can be an exciting and interactive experience. Here's a step-by-step approach:

Introduction (10 minutes):

  • Start by explaining the objectives of the class: to develop extemporaneous speaking skills, critical thinking, research abilities, and effective communication.
  • Share the description of the favorite event, extemporaneous speaking, highlighting its key components.
  • Discuss the importance of gathering knowledge quickly, synthesizing it, forming opinions, building support, and delivering persuasive speeches.

Activity 1 - Quick Knowledge Gathering (15 minutes):

  • Begin with a warm-up activity. Provide students with a random, thought-provoking question.
  • Give them 5 minutes to research and gather relevant information using electronic devices or provided resources.
  • Each student will then present their response in 1-2 minutes. Encourage concise and effective communication.

Activity 2 - Synthesis and Opinion Formation (20 minutes):

  • Introduce a current and controversial topic relevant to the students.
  • Divide students into small groups and assign each group a different perspective on the topic.
  • In 10 minutes, each group should research, synthesize information, form an opinion, and prepare a brief argument supporting their viewpoint.

Activity 3 - Support Building (15 minutes):

  • Have each group share their arguments with the class. Encourage respectful discussion and debate.
  • During the discussion, emphasize the importance of backing opinions with credible evidence, facts, and statistics.

Activity 4 - Extemporaneous Speech Preparation (20 minutes):

  • Assign students a new, thought-provoking question related to the topic discussed.
  • Give them 15 minutes to research and prepare a concise extemporaneous speech. Emphasize the use of credible sources and clear organization.

Activity 5 - Extemporaneous Speech Delivery (15 minutes):

  • Each student delivers their extemporaneous speech within a 2-minute time limit.
  • After each speech, allow for questions and a brief critique from peers, focusing on clarity, persuasiveness, and evidence use.

Reflection (10 minutes):

  • Lead a class discussion on what students learned during the class.
  • Ask them to share their experiences, challenges, and what they found most valuable about extemporaneous speaking.

Homework/Practice (Optional):

  • Assign students extemporaneous speaking topics to research and practice on their own. Encourage them to time themselves and improve their skills.

Dale Carnegie's Magic Formula for Extemporaneous Speaking

Dale Carnegie's "Magic Formula" for extemporaneous speaking is a simple and effective method to help individuals speak confidently and persuasively in impromptu situations. The formula consists of three key elements:

Tell them what you're going to say

Begin your speech or response by providing a clear and concise introduction. State your main message or point upfront to give your audience a sense of direction and purpose. This helps grab their attention and sets the stage for what's to come.

**Say it

In the main body of your speech, elaborate on your main message with supporting points, evidence, examples, and anecdotes. Organize your ideas logically and ensure a smooth flow from one point to the next. Use persuasive language and engage your audience by addressing their interests and concerns.

Tell them what you've said

Conclude your speech by summarizing the key points you've made. Restate your main message and emphasize its significance. This repetition reinforces your message and ensures that your audience remembers your key points.

By following this formula, speakers can structure their extemporaneous speeches effectively, making them more engaging and memorable for their audience.
