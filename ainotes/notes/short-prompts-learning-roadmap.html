Learning Roadmap

Source: AI/Short Prompts/Learning Roadmap.md

1. "Act as a world-class [skill] instructor. I'm a complete beginner with zero experience. Create a 30-day learning roadmap to go from 0 to job-ready. Break it into weeks. Each day should have one focused lesson, one exercise, and one real-world mini project. Use only free resources. No fluff."

2. "You are the best [skill] teacher alive. Explain [complex topic] to me like I'm 12 years old. Use real-world analogies. Then explain it like I'm a college student. Then explain it like I'm a senior professional. Give me the same concept at 3 levels so I understand it deeply, not just on the surface."

3. "I want to learn [skill] but I only have 30 minutes a day. Act as a productivity-focused tutor. Build me a micro-learning plan where each session is exactly 25 minutes of learning + 5 minutes of practice. Cover the 20% of knowledge that gives 80% of results. Pareto principle only."

4. "Act as a strict exam proctor. I've been studying [topic] for 2 weeks. Give me a 20-question test mixing multiple choice, short answer, and real scenario-based problems. Grade my answers after I respond, explain what I got wrong, and tell me exactly what to review."

5. "You are a mentor who learned [skill] the hard way. Tell me the top 10 mistakes beginners make when learning [skill] and how to avoid each one. For each mistake, give a specific example of what it looks like and what to do instead. Be brutally honest. Save me months of wasted time."

6. "Act as a project-based learning coach. I want to learn [skill] by building real things, not watching videos. Give me 5 increasingly difficult projects I can build from scratch. For each project, tell me what concepts it teaches, estimated time, and link to relevant free documentation or tutorials."

7. "Create a flashcard-style study guide for [topic]. Give me 30 question-and-answer pairs that cover the most important concepts. Format each as: Q: [question] → A: [concise answer]. Focus on what actually gets asked in interviews and real-world scenarios, not textbook trivia."
