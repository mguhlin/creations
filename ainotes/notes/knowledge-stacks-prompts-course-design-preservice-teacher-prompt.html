TCEA PreService Teacher Course Outline

Welcome and Introduction

Home:

Welcome to the TCEA PreService Teacher course. One of TCEA’s strategic initiatives is to enhance offerings and outreach for preservice teachers.

This online, self-paced course addresses key areas you need to know to be successful in K-12 schools. What’s more, it blends technology into every aspect in ways that many traditional preservice courses fail to do.

The purpose of this course is to ensure you are ready to teach. It includes how to design effective lessons aligned to national standards, how to teach with high-effect size instructional strategies, and more.

Audience:

The audience for this course includes:

Any preservice teacher who wants to gain practical insights from the field

Include universities

Note: In this document, you will find the outline for two courses. Those courses include:

PreService Teacher, Part 1: This course is focused on critical elements of being a preservice teacher. Those elements include classroom management, lesson design, instructional strategies, and what teaching is about.

PreService Teacher, Part 2: This course addresses ancillary, yet essential, elements of teaching and learning, including special student populations, technology, professional learning networks, legality, ethics, and how to get a job.

3.1.1 Introduction to Classroom Management

A. Importance of Effective Classroom Management

Effective classroom management is foundational for creating an environment where teaching and learning can flourish. It plays a pivotal role for both teachers and students.

INSERT INFOGRAPHIC HERE

Below are some reasons why effective classroom management is so important:

It Promotes a Positive Learning Environment

Classroom management helps in creating a space where all students feel safe, respected, and valued. A positive environment encourages students to participate, ask questions, and express their opinions without fear.

It Maximizes Instructional Time

By minimizing disruptions and streamlining routines, teachers can ensure that classroom time is used efficiently for teaching and learning, maximizing the educational value of each lesson.

It Improves Student Academic Achievement

Research indicates that classrooms with effective management have students who achieve at higher levels. When students are engaged and disruptions are low, they can focus better on the instructional content.

It Supports Social and Emotional Development

A well-managed classroom provides a structure where students can develop important life skills such as self-discipline, conflict resolution, cooperation, and responsibility.

It Reduces Teacher Burnout

Managing a chaotic classroom can be emotionally exhausting for teachers. Effective classroom management practices can lead to reduced stress, higher job satisfaction, and reduced risk of burnout.

It Establishes Consistency and Predictability

Regular routines and clear expectations provide a predictable structure. This consistency can be particularly beneficial for students who may have inconsistent or unpredictable situations outside of school.

It Helps Build Teacher-Student Relationships

When classroom rules are clear and fair, and when teachers respond consistently and compassionately, it lays the foundation for mutual respect and a strong teacher-student bond. This relationship, in turn, positively impacts student motivation and achievement.

It Promotes Student Autonomy

Good classroom management isn't about strict control but rather about teaching students to manage themselves. Over time, students learn to regulate their behavior, leading to greater independence and self-direction.

It Prepares Students for the Future

The skills and behaviors encouraged in a well-managed classroom—such as punctuality, responsibility, collaboration, and respect—prepare students for future educational settings, workplaces, and social interactions.

It Helps the Teacher Use Different Instructional Strategies

Effective classroom management allows the teacher to use a variety of instructional strategies, such as group work, individualized instruction, and project-based learning, by ensuring that transitions are smooth. It demonstrates that students know what's expected of them in different settings.

Ultimately, effective classroom management serves as the foundation upon which meaningful learning experiences can be built. It's not just about enforcing rules, it's about nurturing a space where students and teachers can thrive.

3.1.2 Strategies for Preventing and Resolving Conflicts

A. Recognizing Potential Sources of Conflict in the Classroom

There are many potential sources of conflict among students in a classroom. Some of the most common include:

Differences in Personality: Some students are naturally more outgoing and assertive, while others are more introverted and shy. These differences can sometimes lead to conflict, especially if the students are not able to find a way to work together effectively.

Competition for Resources: When there are limited resources, such as pencils, paper, or computer time, students may compete with each other for these resources. This can lead to conflict, especially if the students feel that they are not being treated fairly.

Bullying: Bullying is a form of aggressive behavior that is intended to harm or intimidate another person. It can take many forms, including physical, verbal, and emotional bullying. Bullying can have a serious impact on the victim, and it is important to address it promptly if it occurs in your classroom.

Cultural Differences: Students from different cultures may have different values and beliefs. This can sometimes lead to conflict, as students may not understand or respect each other's cultures.

Language Barriers: Students who are not fluent in the language of instruction may have difficulty communicating with their classmates and teachers. This can lead to conflict, as students may feel frustrated or left out.

To recognize potential sources of conflict in your classroom, it is important to be aware of the different factors that can contribute to conflict. You can also pay attention to the interactions between your students and look for signs of conflict, such as raised voices, name-calling, or physical aggression. Once you have identified potential sources of conflict, you can take steps to prevent them from escalating.

B. Ways to Prevent Conflict

Here are some specific things you can do to prevent conflict in your classroom:

Set clear expectations and rules for behavior. Make sure that all students know what is expected of them in your classroom. This will help to reduce confusion and conflict.

Be consistent in your enforcement of rules. When you enforce the rules consistently, students will know that you mean business and that they will be held accountable for their actions.

Teach students conflict resolution skills. Help students learn how to communicate effectively, listen to others, and solve problems peacefully.

Create a positive classroom climate. A positive classroom climate is one where students feel safe, respected, and valued. This can help to reduce conflict and promote cooperation.

Be a role model for positive behavior. Students learn by watching the adults in their lives. If you want your students to behave in a certain way, you need to model that behavior yourself.

By taking these steps, you can help to prevent conflict in your classroom and create a more positive learning environment for all students.

3.1.3 Techniques for Establishing and Enforcing Classroom Rules

A. Principles of Effective Rule-Setting

Setting effective classroom rules is crucial. Here are some principles to consider when establishing rules for your classroom:

Keep It Simple.

Limit the number of rules to a manageable amount, usually 3-5. This makes it easier for students to remember and adhere to them.

Be Positive.

Frame rules in positive terms. Focus on the positive behaviors you want your students to display rather than those you don’t. For example, "Raise your hand to speak" instead of "Don't shout out."

Be Specific.

Clearly define what is expected. Instead of a vague rule like "Be respectful," specify what respectful behavior looks like. For example, "Listen when others are speaking."

Involve Students.

Engage students in the rule-setting process. This can increase their sense of ownership and commitment to following the rules.

Be Consistent.

Once rules are set, apply them consistently. Inconsistent enforcement can lead to confusion and perceived unfairness.

Make Them Visible.

Display the rules prominently in the classroom, so students can see them. This will serve as a constant reminder.

Be Flexible.

While consistency is essential, there may be unique situations where flexibility is needed. Be prepared to adapt as the classroom dynamics change.

Teach and Model.

Don't assume students know how to follow the rules. Spend time teaching the desired behaviors and modeling them yourself.

Review the Rules Regularly.

Periodically review the rules with students, especially after breaks or when issues arise. This will reinforce their importance.

Establish Consequences.

Establish clear and appropriate consequences for breaking rules. Ensure that these consequences are fair, consistent, and understood by all students.

Give Positive Reinforcement.

Recognize and reward positive behavior. This can motivate students to adhere to the rules and create a positive classroom culture.

Get Feedback.

Allow for a system where students can voice concerns or provide feedback about the rules. This can help in refining them and addressing any unforeseen issues.

Remember that the primary purpose of classroom rules is to create a safe and conducive learning environment. They should empower students to take responsibility for their behavior and foster mutual respect among everyone in the classroom.

B. Strategies for Communicating and Reinforcing Rules

It is important that you communicate and reinforce the classroom rules. This will help maintain a positive learning environment.

Communicating the Rules

By communicating the rules you are ensuring that students understand, value, and adhere to the established rules of the classroom. Below are some strategies to ensure that rules are clearly communicated:

Begin the school year by introducing the rules on the first day. This sets clear expectations from the start.

Create visual displays or posters that list the classroom rules. Display them where students can see them. This way, students will be able to refer to them regularly.

Have discussions with your students about the rules. Get their input and ask them why they think each rule is essential.

Have students role-play different scenarios of both following and breaking the rules. This will help them understand the importance of each rule.

Start each day or week with a brief review of the rules. This reminder will keep the rules fresh in students' minds.

Use games or quizzes to test students' understanding of the rules. For instance, a quick quiz or a game that revolves around classroom rules can be both fun and educational. Check out these 15 fun ways to review classroom rules and procedures.

Be sure to communicate classroom rules to parents. This can be done at parent-teacher conferences or by emailing handouts. When parents are aware, they can help reinforce the same behaviors at home. In addition, clearly communicate any ongoing behavior issues with parents.

By communicating the rules, you are ensuring that students understand their importance. When students understand and value the rules, they're more likely to adhere to them.

Reinforcing the Rules

Establish clear and appropriate consequences for breaking rules. Ensure that these consequences are known and understood by students. This will ensure that students know what to expect if they don’t adhere to the rules.

Always enforce the rules consistently. Inconsistent enforcement can lead to confusion and a perception of unfairness.

Address rule-breaking behavior immediately when it occurs. This sends a clear message that such behavior is not tolerated.

Create a behavior contract that outlines expected behaviors, consequences for breaking the rules, and rewards for improved behavior. Have students sign the behavior contract.

Praise and reward students who consistently follow the rules. This can be done through verbal praise, stickers, points systems, or other rewards.

Have student ambassadors or leaders who can help reinforce the rules among their peers.

Use classroom management apps and websites to reinforce rules. For example, Class Dojo is an app that allows teachers to give points for positive behavior.

As the teacher, always model the behavior you expect from your students. Your actions set the tone for the classroom.

If a student repeatedly breaks a rule, have a private conversation with them to understand the reasons behind their behavior and discuss potential solutions.

Instead of just punishing rule-breaking behavior, use restorative practices to help students understand the impact of their actions.

Be sure to document incidents. Keep a record of rule-breaking incidents. This documentation can be useful for parent-teacher conferences or to track patterns of behavior.

If you're facing challenges with enforcing rules, find support from colleagues, school counselors, or administrators. They might offer new strategies or perspectives.

3.1.5 Additional Strategies to Help with Classroom Management

A. Non-verbal Communication Strategies

Effective classroom management often hinges on the teacher's ability to communicate expectations, not only verbally but also non-verbally. Here are some non-verbal communication strategies that can aid in classroom management:

Eye Contact

Making eye contact can be a powerful tool for gaining attention, showing you're engaged, or addressing misbehavior without words.

Facial Expressions

Your facial expressions can convey a range of emotions, from approval to disappointment, which can influence student behavior.

Gestures

Use hand signals or gestures for common instructions. For example, raising your hand to signal silence or pointing to the clock to remind of time constraints.

Proximity

Simply moving closer to a student or a group can deter potential misbehavior.

Body Language

Maintain an open and relaxed posture to appear approachable. Stand straight and firm when trying to convey seriousness.

Classroom Arrangement

The way desks and chairs are arranged can influence student behavior. Think about how you want your classroom arrangement to look.

Signals

Develop signals with the class for common instructions. For example, turning off the lights might signal it's time to pay attention to the front.

Nods and Shakes

Simple nods can affirm positive behavior, while head shakes can indicate disapproval without verbalizing it.

Thumbs Up/Down

A quick thumbs up can show approval, while a thumbs down can indicate something is incorrect or not acceptable.

Remember, consistency is key. Whether you're using verbal or non-verbal strategies, being consistent in your communication helps students understand and meet your expectations. Over time, many of these strategies can become ingrained routines, making classroom management smoother and more intuitive.
