My Dear Students,

I just wanted to take a moment to let you know what I am looking for in these posts. I am a specialist in teaching with AI. Although I teach with an eCore template for this course, I have a very clear understanding of how, exactly, I will use their rubrics when I see an inappropriate use of AI.

Of course, I can't prove someone used AI. That is impossible, and I don't use AI detectors as they have been shown to be wildly ineffective. So, when I look at assignments, I am looking for a few things that AI can't do:

VOICE: Everything you write should sound like you. It is OK to use AI in my courses, but it is never OK to sound like you used AI. You must read everything ALOUD and listen to what you are saying. Is there a word you don't know or you can't pronounce? Replace it! Is there a phrase that doesn't sound like you? Reword it! You must make sure you understand what voice is, and why it is so important—especially as we move into a world where human voice will be valued for its unique character and insight.

TONE AND AUDIENCE: Who are you writing to? If you know you are writing to peers, for example, you had better sound like a college freshman writing to peers. I have been teaching for 30 years. I can easily tell if you sound like a college freshman--can you? If you can't, stay away from AI.

EVIDENCE-BASED WRITING: You must provide evidence for what you are writing, and that evidence must be integrated into what you are writing. General commentary without examples and vague language without evidence are not acceptable.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY. AI will hallucinate (that is the technical term) when it doesn't know something. AI will make up facts, quotes, and even entire books that don't exist. NEVER write about something you don't know because you won't know if AI has hallucinated things--but I will. If I know something isn't factual and you have put it into your work, you have violated academic integrity--not because you used AI, but because you presented inaccurate or falsified information.

TRANSPARENCY. If you used AI, please be transparent about it. Tell me you used AI, how you used AI, and why you used AI. If you aren't using it correctly, and you are transparent, I will assume you just need some pointers to make your use more ethical and responsible, and you can redo it. If you weren't transparent, I will assume you meant to cheat, and I will not let you redo it.

I don't mind if you use AI to brainstorm, enhance, correct, or assist you—but never use AI as a substitute for your legitimate work. I am sneaky. I never have to prove you used AI, I only have to prove you didn't meet the assignment requirements.

Dr. Kassorla, University System of Georgia's eCore classes
