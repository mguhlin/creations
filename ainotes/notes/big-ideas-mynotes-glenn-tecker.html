Glenn Tecker (an ASAE think tank guy that I admire):

As a futurist and a strategist, I recently had the opportunity to work with a multidisciplinary group of Space Scientists, Their group is contracted to the U.S. federal government. Members are working on issues like outposts on the far side of the moon, interstellar communications, and rapidly evolving insight into the continuous evolution of the Universe. As you might surmise, AI is integral to the multifaceted work in which their members are engaged.

One of the topics of discussion had to do with an impending "bubble" in AI development. This bubble is not about the dynamics of huge investments in the various competing platforms, or the hype being disseminated by vendors and investors seeking to raise additional capital. The Gartner Consulting Group recently declared that AI was entering its famous (or infamous) "trough of disillusionment." The expectation of a "bubble" in development was one of the considerations in placing AI in this stage of Gartner's technologies life cycle scheme. The insight produced by discussion with these leading scientists may be useful to future focused association professionals..

Discussion of this AI bubble has recently appeared in several publications including the Journal Nature and Science. The issue has to do with the training sets needed to provide the essential data to AI large language models like ChatGPT and Pilot.

As most folks choosing to read this post probably know, AI is really not intelligent as we appreciate that in humans. It is simply able to run many millions of simulations very rapidly using new chips such as those manufactured by NVIDIA. So, on a given question or problem these AI routines attempt to improve the probability of a correct or informative answer through these simulations.

But the AI is only as good as the training set from which it comes. We've heard about "hallucinations" which result in part from training sets being incomplete such that these AI routines make things up to fill in the gaps (interpolation between data sets). The bubble comes from the fact that we are quickly running out of data to train these AI routines. The internet has been scraped, NY Times and newspapers have been downloaded (maybe illegally), etc. Without new data, the progress of AI is going to be limited – thus, the bubble may burst quickly in how much improvement we can make on AI.

Computer scientists have suggested that AI itself could create new training sets by extrapolating existing data. But an article in Nature from several weeks ago indicated that so far this has bombed out. As the AI training sets are created via AI, they are less and less accurate and become just wrong. This, too, is not encouraging and may burst the bubble.
