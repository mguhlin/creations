Essential Learning Expectations (ELEs) for Teachers

"The most powerful single influence enhancing achievement is feedback." - John Hattie
Overview

The Essential Learning Expectations for Teachers are designed to guide educators in developing the skills and knowledge necessary for effective, modern teaching. These expectations are grounded in research on best practices in education, including John Hattie's visible learning principles, which emphasize the importance of teacher clarity and feedback. They also incorporate findings from studies on technology integration, culturally responsive teaching, and the development of critical thinking skills. By focusing on areas such as tech integration, digital literacy, inclusive teaching, relationship building, and critical thinking, these ELEs aim to equip teachers with the tools they need to create engaging, effective, and inclusive learning environments that prepare students for their future.

Guiding Questions for Teachers
  1. How effectively am I integrating technology into my teaching practices?

  1. How am I creating an inclusive learning environment that respects diversity?
  1. What strategies am I using to build positive relationships with my students?
  1. How am I fostering critical thinking skills in my lessons?

TCEA Style Guide

AVOID the following:
  • Ampersand (&) use; instead use “and”
  • Placement of two spaces after a period at the end of a sentence
  • Overuse of exclamation marks (!)
  • Typing the full hyperlink and then linking it (https://tcea.org )
  • Putting periods at the ends of bulleted or numbered lists unless they are complete sentences
DO the following
  • Use the Oxford comma
  • Capitalize if the product being taught is normally capitalized
  • Hyperlink text of the link rather than adding the link itself (**TCEA Courses**)
  • Bold links
  • Explain common abbreviations like LMS, IEP, etc.
  • Write out numbers up to ten (“Take three of these….” instead of “Take 3 of these…”)
  • Capitalize the first letter of each bullet or numbered list item
  • If a punctuation mark is needed to separate items surrounded by quotation marks, put it inside the quotation marks. (Save the file and click "Open." instead of Save the File and click "Open".)
  • Rely on second person (e.g. “you” perspective and “you, your, yours, yourself, yourselves”)
