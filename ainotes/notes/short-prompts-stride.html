Here’s an age-appropriate model acronym for grades 3–10 that captures evidence-based processes: “STRIDE”

STRIDE Model

S – Scan the Problem:

• Look for important numbers, keywords, and questions.

• Use a strategy like circling or underlining to organize information visually.

T – Think About Connections:

• Connect the problem to what you already know.

• Visualize or draw the situation to build understanding.

R – Reflect on the Question:

• Ask yourself: What is the problem asking me to find or do?

• Rephrase the question in your own words for clarity.

I – Identify a Strategy:

• Decide what steps or operations will help solve the problem (e.g., addition, subtraction, or creating a diagram).

• Use tools like graphic organizers or charts if needed.

D – Do the Work:

• Solve step by step, checking each calculation or action.

• Write down your reasoning to track your thinking process.

E – Evaluate Your Answer:

• Check: Does the answer make sense in the context of the problem?

• If something seems off, review the steps or try a different approach.

STRIDE Implementation Guide

  1. Introduce STRIDE with Explicit Teaching

• Grade 3-5: Use relatable examples (e.g., solving a simple puzzle or story problem). Use visual aids like posters or digital slides.

• Grade 6-10: Incorporate real-world problems (e.g., budgeting or measuring). Encourage students to create personal examples of each step.

  1. Step-by-Step STRIDE Activities
  1. S – Scan the Problem:

• Teach students to circle key numbers, underline keywords, and highlight the main question.

• Use practice problems and model how to scan effectively on the board or a digital whiteboard.

  1. T – Think About Connections:

• Use questioning prompts: “Have you seen a problem like this before?” or “What do you know that can help here?”

• Pair students for think-aloud activities or brainstorming.

  1. R – Reflect on the Question:

• Guide students to restate the question in simpler terms or use sentence starters like:

• “This problem is asking me to…”

• “To solve this, I need to find…”

  1. I – Identify a Strategy:

• Provide a “toolbox” of strategies (e.g., draw a picture, use a table, write an equation).

• Role-play “strategy selection” in groups, choosing and explaining the best approach.

  1. D – Do the Work:

• Emphasize neat and clear steps while solving.

• Practice solving in pairs with peer feedback.

  1. E – Evaluate Your Answer:

• Teach students to ask:

• “Does my answer make sense?”

• “Did I answer the question?”

• Use rubrics or checklists for self-assessment.

  1. Reflective Practices

• Incorporate journals or digital tools like Padlet to reflect on how STRIDE helped solve problems.

• Use sentence starters:

• “The step that helped me most was…”

• “Next time, I will improve by…”

  1. Integration Across Subjects

• Math: Use STRIDE for word problems and equations.

• Science: Apply it to experimental analysis or hypotheses.

• ELA: Analyze comprehension questions with the steps.
