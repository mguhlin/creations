Adapting the findings from “The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking” using the SOLO Taxonomy instead of Bloom’s Taxonomy, we can restructure the study’s conclusions based on progressive levels of understanding and critical thinking development:

SOLO Taxonomy-Aligned Outline of “The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking”

  1. Pre-Structural – Minimal Understanding of AI’s Impact on Thinking

• Users may not recognize AI’s cognitive effects

• Some users treat AI as a simple automation tool, not considering how it shapes their thinking.

• Lack of awareness: Users may not engage in deep questioning or verification.

• Cognitive Offloading: Some workers over-rely on AI without assessing what it replaces in their reasoning.

• AI’s Role in This Stage

• AI can be used for basic information retrieval but requires human intervention for deeper understanding.

• Example: Users accept AI-generated text without critically evaluating its accuracy or coherence.

  1. Uni-Structural – Surface-Level AI Engagement

• Basic Understanding of AI’s Functionality

• Users understand how to prompt AI for responses but do not evaluate its credibility deeply.

• Critical thinking occurs in isolated moments, rather than being applied systematically.

• Users follow AI-generated suggestions but struggle to modify or challenge them.

• AI’s Role in This Stage

• AI is used for structured guidance, such as brainstorming or summarizing information.

• Example: A knowledge worker uses AI for drafting emails but relies entirely on its suggestions without editing.

  1. Multi-Structural – Understanding AI’s Role, but Lacking Synthesis

• Users engage in selective AI verification

• Workers recognize AI’s limitations but struggle to integrate their own expertise.

• Overconfidence in AI outputs can lead to accepting incorrect or biased responses.

• Some knowledge workers fact-check AI inconsistently, only verifying outputs when errors are obvious.

• AI’s Role in This Stage

• AI supports comparison and multi-perspective analysis.

• Users start employing frameworks like SIFT for AI evaluation but do not always apply them consistently.

• Example: A researcher asks AI for sources but only cross-checks when the answer seems suspicious.

  1. Relational – AI and Humans Working in Tandem for Critical Thinking

• Users recognize AI’s strengths and weaknesses

• They actively verify AI’s responses rather than assuming correctness.

• Higher-order skills emerge: Synthesizing AI-generated content with domain expertise.

• Users develop AI literacy, balancing efficiency with cognitive effort in reasoning.

• AI’s Role in This Stage

• AI serves as a collaborator rather than a knowledge source.

• AI is used for brainstorming, analyzing patterns, and prompting deeper reflection.

• Example: A lawyer drafts an argument using AI, then critically revises and improves logical coherence.

  1. Extended Abstract – Mastery of AI-Augmented Critical Thinking

• Users treat AI as an innovation partner

• AI is not just a tool but a cognitive amplifier, enabling creative and novel problem-solving.

• Users can transfer AI-generated insights into new domains.

• AI usage is intentional, with clear ethical considerations and domain-specific expertise.

• AI’s Role in This Stage

• AI is leveraged for rapid prototyping and hypothesis testing.

• Users engage in reflective practice, using AI outputs to challenge assumptions and develop new frameworks.

• Example: A scientist uses AI to generate hypotheses, then tests them with rigorous independent analysis.

Key Takeaways Using SOLO Taxonomy

  1. AI’s influence on critical thinking depends on the user’s SOLO stage.
  1. Lower SOLO levels (Pre-Structural, Uni-Structural) rely on AI without questioning, increasing risks of cognitive offloading.
  1. Higher SOLO levels (Relational, Extended Abstract) use AI strategically, integrating it with critical evaluation.
  1. Designing AI for knowledge workers should scaffold users from surface learning (Multi-Structural) to deeper engagement (Relational & Extended Abstract).
  1. Future research should focus on training strategies to help workers progress through these SOLO stages with AI literacy development.

Final Thoughts

This SOLO-aligned structure shows that AI does not inherently diminish critical thinking—instead, its effect depends on how users engage with it. Supporting knowledge workers in moving toward Relational and Extended Abstract thinking is key to ensuring AI enhances, rather than replaces, critical cognition.
