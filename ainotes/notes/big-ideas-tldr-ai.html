Here's the TLDR:

📚 Study 1: How ChatGPT impacts student engagement from a systematic review and meta-analysis study (2024) > AI improves cognitive & emotional engagement, especially in struggling students. However, it limits the development of higher order skills compared with traditional learning methods.

📚 Study 2: The Impact of Large Language Models on Programming Education and Student Learning Outcomes (2024) > AI enhances the speed of task performance but hinders foundational skill development, especially in beginners.

📚 Study 3: Let GPT be a Math Tutor: Teaching Math Word Problem Solvers with Customized Exercise Generation (2023) > Customised AI exercises can improve problem-solving and skill retention, but "over supportive" generic AI tools negatively impact long term retention and higher-order skill development.

📚 Study 4: AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking (2024) > Cognitive offloading to AI reduces learners' critical thinking engagement (r = -0.75) compared with traditional methods.

📚 Study 5: Beware of Metacognitive Laziness: Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence on Motivation, Self-Regulated Learning, and Performance (2024) >

AI tools boosts learning confidence but negatively impacts actual learning outcomes.

Practical Implications:

→Limit the Use of Generic AI in Foundational Learning: During the early stages of learning, focus on manual problem-solving and hands-on practice to build strong conceptual knowledge and foundational skills before incorporating AI tools.

→ Treat Generic AI as a Learning Activity, Not a Teacher: Use tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Co-Pilot & Gemini for practice, problem sets and exploration. Encourage learners to engage with AI-generated outputs by solving problems, analysing solutions, and critiquing its responses to develop deeper understanding and critical thinking skills.

→ Advocate for Pedagogy-First AI Models & Tools: Be wary of the risks of using generic AI for learning. Seek out "pedagogy-first" AI systems that are designed by both engineers and educators, with evidence-based learning principles at their core.

For more info, including a vision of what pedagogy-first AI systems might look like, check out the full blog post.
