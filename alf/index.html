The Augmented K–16 AI Learning Framework

A practical framework for K–16 AI adoption that links professional roles to product-oriented tools, print-ready outputs, and responsible implementation.

Tool names are examples only. Substitute locally approved tools based on privacy agreements, licensing, accessibility, and policy.

Framework at a Glance

Human-centered

AI should reduce routine workload and increase time for feedback, relationships, discussion, and creation.

Pedagogy-first

AI use should connect to SOLO, ACE, scaffolding, inquiry, feedback, and visible thinking.

Product-oriented

Each scenario tool now generates a printable plan, checklist, rubric, grouping map, or implementation product.

Instructional, Privacy, and AI Adoption Links

  • SOLO Taxonomy — levels of learner understanding from surface to deeper conceptual transfer.
  • ACE: Articulate, Connect, Extend — reflection prompts for explaining, connecting, and extending learning.
  • Visible Learning / High-Effect Size Instructional Strategies — teacher clarity, feedback, formative evaluation, and related strategies.
  • TCEA PROTECT Rubric — privacy and AI safety lens for evaluating tools and workflows.
  • SHINE Framework — responsible AI adoption with safety, human judgment, instructional purpose, and ethics.
  • Five Phases of School AI Adoption — AI Secured, Exploration, Pilot Programs, Unification, and Everywhere.

Five Phases of School District AI Adoption

1. AI Secured

AI tools are restricted or blocked while safety, policy, and communication questions are addressed.

2. AI Exploration

Limited exploration begins with guardrails, informal pilots, and early parent communication.

3. AI Pilot Programs

Selected classrooms test approved AI uses with clearer guidance, accounts, and learning goals.

4. AI Unification

Policies, account management, curriculum use, and guidance become coordinated and consistent.

5. AI Everywhere

AI is integrated into policies, procedures, curriculum, teaching, learning, and community education.

Actionable Starting Points

  1. Identify your adoption phase: Use the Five Phases of School AI Adoption to determine your current state.
  2. Start with policy and privacy: Use the TCEA PROTECT Rubric as a review lens.
  3. Use a responsible adoption frame: Apply the SHINE Framework to keep AI safe, purposeful, transparent, and human-centered.
  4. Design for process: Assess prompts, revisions, critique, reflection, oral defense, and evidence of thinking.
  5. Compress screen time: Use brief AI interactions to prepare students for discussion, writing, building, testing, and collaboration.
  6. Keep humans in charge: AI can assist, sort, draft, and scaffold, but educators remain responsible for judgment, care, context, and final decisions.
